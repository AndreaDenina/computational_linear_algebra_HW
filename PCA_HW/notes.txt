The standard scaler generates some drawbacks, in particular features with high variance get compressed and those with low variance get stecthed.

Almost everyone (82%) answered 5 to Music, so the question tells you very little about how people differ. After scaling, though:

One point means different things.
One point on the Music scale is worth 1.61 units, while one point on Pets is worth only 0.64. 
A small difference in Music now counts about 2.5 times more than the same difference in Pets.

A few answers become extreme values.
The 3 people who answered 1 to Music get −6.03, a value far bigger than anything Pets can produce.

The noise gets amplified too. (Not really a problem)
Whether someone answers 4 or 5 to "I enjoy music" may depend more on their mood that day than on who they are. 
The scaler can't tell real information from noise; 
it just raises every feature to variance 1.


The variance that carried information is gone. 
Before scaling, a high variance meant "this question splits people into groups", which is exactly what you want for profiling customers. 
After scaling, every question has variance 1, so that information is lost.

The PCA has a harder time compressing. The total variance is spread evenly over 93 features, so each PC explains less. 
The cumulative curve rises more slowly than for responses_ft_enc.

Rare answers can pull the PCs. Extreme values like −6 have a large effect on variances and covariances,
so a handful of people can influence the direction of some PCs.


Why scale anyway? In this dataset almost every feature is already on the same 1–5 scale. So the usual reason for standardizing,
features measured in different units like kg and cm, is weak here. The real reasons are:

- the 3 categorical features have a different range (1–3 or 1–4);
- without scaling, a few high-variance features would dominate the PCs;
- with scaling, the PCs reflect correlations between questions rather than how widely each question was answered.

Without scaling the PCA finds principal components that describe the questions with sparser answers 
instead we want to find correlations between the features
in order to highlight relations. 

**Pros.** Standard scaling gives every feature the same weight, so the PCA works on the correlation matrix: 
each PC with variance above 1 comes only from groups of correlated questions, 
which is what we need to identify profiles. It also prevents high-variance features (e.g., `Pets`, 2.43) 
from dominating the first PCs, and removes the effect of the different ranges of the encoded categorical features (1–3 or 1–4 vs 1–5).

**Cons.** Low-variance features are amplified together with their noise: in `Music`, 
where most people answered 5, a rare answer of 1 becomes −6.03. The information carried by the original variances 
(which questions split people the most) is lost, and since the total variance is spread evenly, 
more PCs are needed to reach the same share of it.
